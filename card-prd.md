# Tetra Card Positioning Foundation

## Core Read

Crypto cards are already crowded. Coinbase, Crypto.com, Gemini, Bybit, ether.fi, Gnosis Pay, Rain, and others all offer some version of: spend crypto or stablecoins through a Visa/Mastercard card, often with rewards.

Tetra should not lead with:

- Best rewards
- Highest cashback
- Spend crypto anywhere
- Another exchange-linked card
- A complicated staking or tier program

Those are crowded and easy to copy.

## The Real Opportunity

A basic card is basic. Consumers do not only choose cards for utility. They choose cards because the card says something about how they live, spend, travel, save, invest, or want to be perceived.

The card should make Tetra feel like more than an app. It should make the account feel tangible.

The strongest wedge:

**Your stablecoins earn until you spend them.**

This makes the card the Spend layer of Tetra's broader product:

**Trade globally. Earn every day. Spend anytime.**

## Consumer Behavior Lens

People are attracted to cards for a few reasons:

- **Utility:** it solves a daily spending problem.
- **Status:** it signals taste, access, or wealth.
- **Progression:** tiers make users feel they are leveling up.
- **Control:** limits, buffers, and visibility make money feel managed.
- **Lifestyle fit:** travel, global work, trading, or premium access.
- **Identity:** the card becomes a symbol of belonging to a financial culture.

Tetra should not think of the card as one object. It should think of the card as a product surface for different user identities.

## Card Direction Ideas

### Tetra Core

For getting started.

The simple card for digital dollar spending. It should be clean, free or low-friction, and focused on making the user's stablecoin balance usable.

Promise: **Start spending from your Tetra account.**

### Tetra Travel

For global users, remote workers, and frequent travelers.

This card should emphasize international spend, FX clarity, ATM access, travel limits, and the feeling of having digital dollars that work across borders.

Promise: **Take your digital dollars anywhere.**

### Tetra Yield

For stablecoin savers.

This card is built around the spendable yield idea. Users keep most funds earning while maintaining a spend buffer for everyday use.

Promise: **Keep earning until you spend.**

### Tetra Trader

For active market users.

This card connects spending to trading behavior. Profits, idle margin, or unused capital can return to the account, earn, and stay spendable.

Promise: **One balance for markets and real life.**

### Tetra Metal

For premium users who want the product to feel physical, durable, and high-status.

This does not need to be only about material. Metal should represent higher limits, cleaner support, better design, and a stronger account experience.

Promise: **A heavier card for a more serious account.**

### Tetra Private

For high-balance users.

This should not just mean black-card aesthetics. It should mean higher limits, priority support, tailored onboarding, better treasury/yield education, and more personal account management.

Promise: **Private access for serious digital dollar users.**

## Recommended Positioning

Primary narrative:

**Spend from the same balance that earns.**

Supporting idea:

Tetra lets users hold digital dollars, earn on idle balances, trade global markets, and spend through a card from one self-custodial account. The user should not feel like they are moving money between a wallet, yield app, exchange, and card.

Short copy options:

- **Your stablecoins earn until you spend them.**
- **The card for spendable yield.**
- **Real-world spending for your digital dollar account.**
- **One balance for trading, earning, and spending.**
- **A card for money that does not sit still.**

## Product Implications

For beta, the card should clearly show:

- Total stablecoin balance
- Amount earning
- Amount available to spend
- Spend buffer
- Fees, limits, and transaction history

The important product idea is not "card tiers" alone. It is giving users a reason to see themselves in a card type.

Tetra should explore tiers or card types around behavior:

- Core for access
- Travel for global utility
- Yield for stablecoin savers
- Trader for active market users
- Metal for premium identity
- Private for high-balance support

Rewards can exist, but they should be secondary. The main reason to care should be that Tetra makes stablecoins useful without making them idle.

## Strategic Takeaway

Most crypto cards are cards attached to an exchange, wallet, rewards program, or infrastructure provider.

Tetra can stand out by making the card proof of the account narrative:

**Money should stay productive until the moment it is used.**

The card should attract users through both function and identity. It should answer:

**What kind of financial life does this card help me live?**
