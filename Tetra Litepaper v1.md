# Tetra Litepaper v1

## One Account To Trade, Earn, And Spend Globally

Tetra is a self-custodial stablecoin neobank for global users.

It is built for people who want more from digital dollars than a balance sitting in a wallet or on an exchange. With Tetra, stablecoins become the base account for trading global markets, earning on idle capital, and spending when the real world calls.

The product promise is simple:

**Trade globally. Earn every day. Spend anytime.**

Tetra brings three financial behaviors into one on-chain account:

- **Trade** crypto, RWAs, stocks, ETFs, and other global markets.
- **Earn** on idle stablecoin balances when capital is not actively deployed.
- **Spend** from the same account through real-world card utility.

Tetra is not another wallet, exchange, yield farm, or crypto card. It is the account layer that connects those behaviors into one capital loop.

## Why Tetra Exists

Crypto opened global financial markets. Stablecoins gave users digital dollars that can move quickly, settle on-chain, and travel across borders.

But the user experience is still fragmented.

If someone wants to trade, they may keep capital on an exchange. If they want yield, they may move funds into a DeFi protocol. If they want to spend, they may need to off-ramp through a bank or card provider. If they want self-custody, they may need to manage wallets, gas, bridges, approvals, chains, protocols, and risk by themselves.

The infrastructure is powerful. The account experience is broken.

Tetra exists to make digital dollars useful across the full financial lifecycle:

1. Capital can move into market opportunities.
2. Idle balances can keep working.
3. Funds can be spent without breaking the account experience.

The long-term opportunity is to make Tetra the self-custodial account where global users manage capital, not just store crypto.

## The Problem

Most crypto products solve one job at a time.

Exchanges are built for trading. Wallets are built for custody. DeFi protocols are built for on-chain yield. Crypto cards are built for spending. Each product can be useful, but users are forced to move between them.

That creates five problems.

### 1. Trading Capital Is Fragmented

Users who want exposure to crypto, tokenized assets, stocks, ETFs, or other global markets often need separate accounts, interfaces, venues, and rails. Capital gets spread across products instead of staying in one usable account.

### 2. Idle Capital Goes Underused

Stablecoins often sit unused between trades, transfers, or off-ramp events. Users who want yield must leave their primary account, compare protocols, evaluate risk, and manage positions manually.

### 3. Spending Breaks The Flow

Stablecoins are liquid on-chain, but they are not always easy to use in everyday life. Spending often means selling assets, withdrawing funds, waiting for settlement, and moving money through a bank or card provider.

### 4. Self-Custody Is Too Operational

Self-custody gives users more control, but the current experience asks them to think like infrastructure operators. Seed phrases, gas, bridges, chains, approvals, and protocol risk create friction before users reach the actual value.

### 5. Convenience Often Requires Surrendering Control

Centralized platforms can feel easier, but users often give up custody, transparency, and portability. On-chain systems can offer more control, but they are harder to use.

Tetra is built around a different belief: users should be able to access global markets, earn on digital dollars, and spend from one account without giving up the core benefits of self-custody.

## The Tetra Vision

Tetra is a self-custodial account for active capital.

The core loop is:

1. A user funds Tetra with stablecoins.
2. Capital can be deployed into global trading opportunities.
3. Idle balances can earn through curated yield opportunities.
4. Funds remain available for real-world spending.
5. Trading gains or unused funds can return to the account and continue working.

In short:

**Trade when opportunity appears. Earn while capital waits. Spend when you need to.**

This is the difference between a wallet and an account.

A wallet stores assets. An account organizes what users can do with their money.

Tetra's goal is to become the primary self-custodial account for users who want digital dollars, market access, productive idle capital, and real-world utility in one place.

## Product Pillars

Tetra is organized around three connected product pillars: Trade, Earn, and Spend.

These are not separate products. They are one financial loop inside the same account.

## Trade

Trade is Tetra's main product offer.

Tetra gives users a self-custodial account for accessing global market opportunities from digital dollars. Instead of moving capital between exchanges, wallets, apps, and off-chain accounts, users should be able to manage opportunity capital from one place.

The Trade pillar is expected to include access to:

- Crypto assets.
- RWAs.
- Stocks.
- ETFs.
- Other global market products as supported over time.

Tetra should not feel like a standalone trading terminal bolted onto a wallet. Trade is the center of the account experience: the place where users can deploy capital when they see opportunity.

Earn and Spend make that trading account more useful. When capital is not in a position, it can potentially earn. When users need liquidity in the real world, funds can be spent from the same account.

The trading experience should make Tetra feel like the financial home base for global market access.

## Earn

Earn makes idle capital productive.

Trading is not constant. Users wait between opportunities. They keep stablecoin balances ready for the next move. In most products, that capital sits still unless users manually move it somewhere else.

Tetra's Earn layer is designed to make productive idle capital a native part of the account.

The Earn experience should be simple:

- Hold supported stablecoins in Tetra.
- View available yield opportunities.
- Understand the source and risk of yield before opting in.
- Keep visibility into balances, yield, and availability.
- Move funds back into trading or spending when needed, subject to product rules, liquidity, and provider conditions.

Tetra will not position yield as guaranteed, risk-free, or equivalent to a bank savings account. Yield in crypto can be variable and may depend on third-party protocols, market conditions, smart contracts, liquidity, and regulatory availability.

The goal is not to hide that complexity. The goal is to make the workflow easier while making the risks clear.

## Spend

Spend brings Tetra into the real world.

Users should not need to break their financial flow every time they want to use stablecoins outside crypto. Tetra's Spend layer is designed around continuity: the same account used for trading and earning should also support real-world utility.

The core Spend idea is:

**Your money works until the moment you use it.**

For beta, Spend is expected to center on card-based utility. Availability, supported regions, eligible users, fees, limits, and card partner details will depend on the final launch configuration and compliance requirements.

Spend matters because it makes Tetra useful between trades and outside crypto-native environments. It turns the account from a market-access product into a daily capital account.

## The Account Layer

The Account layer is what makes Trade, Earn, and Spend feel connected.

Without a strong account layer, users are left with separate products: one app to trade, one protocol to earn, one wallet to hold, one provider to spend.

Tetra's account layer should abstract complexity around:

- Wallet creation and access.
- Supported chains and assets.
- Stablecoin balances.
- Trading access.
- Yield opt-in and management.
- Spending availability.
- Transaction history.
- Risk and disclosure visibility.

The goal is to preserve the benefits of self-custody while reducing the operational burden that makes crypto difficult for mainstream users.

Self-custody should not mean users are forced to manage infrastructure. It should mean users have more control over a better financial account.

## Who Tetra Is For

Tetra is built for global users who want digital dollars to do more than sit still.

The early user segments include:

- **Active traders** who want global market access without constantly moving capital between accounts.
- **Stablecoin holders** who want idle balances to stay useful between market opportunities.
- **Crypto-native users** who already use wallets, exchanges, and DeFi but want a cleaner financial hub.
- **Global earners and savers** who want access to digital dollars and on-chain financial tools.
- **Users in banking-constrained markets** who need financial tools that are more global, portable, and available than local alternatives.

Tetra is not trying to be a generic crypto super app for everyone on day one.

The first user promise is sharper:

**A self-custodial account where you can trade global markets, earn on idle stablecoins, and spend from the same balance.**

## Why Stablecoin Neobanking

Stablecoins are becoming the base money layer for crypto users. They are how people hold digital dollars, move capital between markets, and wait between opportunities.

But stablecoins alone are not an account.

A stablecoin neobank should feel familiar in the ways users care about:

- One account.
- Clear balance.
- Fast access.
- Useful spending.
- Simple actions.

It should also be meaningfully different from a traditional financial app:

- Self-custodial account design.
- Digital dollar access.
- On-chain settlement.
- Global market connectivity.
- Composable trading and yield infrastructure.

The point is not to copy a bank. The point is to build the financial account that digital dollars make possible.

The goals of the soft launch are:

- Introduce Tetra publicly.
- Establish the stablecoin neobank narrative.
- Explain the Trade, Earn, and Spend pillars.
- Publish the litepaper.
- Collect waitlist demand.
- Begin public education ahead of beta.

### Beta Launch

The beta is expected to follow after the soft launch period.

At beta, Tetra is expected to include the major pillars:

- Trade.
- Earn.
- Spend.

Exact asset support, stablecoin support, yield providers, trading scope, card availability, regional eligibility, fees, and limits will be confirmed closer to beta launch.

The priority for beta is to prove that the account loop works: users can bring stablecoins into Tetra, trade global markets, earn on idle balances, and spend through real-world utility from the same account.

## Design Principles

Tetra's product decisions should follow five principles.

### 1. Trade Is The Center Of The Account

Tetra should make global market access the main reason users care. Earn and Spend should strengthen that account, not distract from it.

### 2. One Account, Not Separate Apps

Trade, Earn, and Spend should feel connected. The user should understand how money moves between holding, trading, earning, and spending.

### 3. Self-Custody With Better UX

Tetra should reduce the friction of wallets and on-chain interactions without removing the user's core control over assets.

### 4. Yield With Transparency

Yield should be explained clearly: source, variability, risks, provider dependencies, and limitations. Tetra should avoid guaranteed-yield language.

### 5. Real-World Utility

Digital dollars become more valuable when they can be used outside crypto. Spend is not an add-on. It is part of the account's core utility.

## Risk And Disclosure Overview

This litepaper is for informational purposes only. It is not investment, financial, legal, or tax advice.

Tetra is not a bank. Tetra accounts should not be described as bank accounts, savings accounts, deposits, or FDIC-insured accounts. Yield should not be described as guaranteed, fixed, or risk-free.

Users should understand the risks of using crypto products, including:

- **Market risk:** trading crypto, RWAs, stocks, ETFs, and other products can involve volatility, losses, leverage risk, liquidity risk, and changing market conditions.
- **Stablecoin risk:** stablecoins can lose their peg, face reserve issues, experience liquidity stress, or become unavailable in certain markets.
- **Smart contract risk:** on-chain systems can contain bugs, vulnerabilities, or economic design failures.
- **Protocol and yield risk:** yield may depend on third-party protocols, borrowers, liquidity conditions, market demand, and other factors outside Tetra's full control.
- **Custody and wallet risk:** self-custodial systems require users to protect account access and understand transaction approvals.
- **Oracle and data risk:** DeFi and trading systems can rely on third-party data sources that may fail, lag, or be manipulated.
- **Bridge and network risk:** supported networks and bridges can experience congestion, censorship, exploits, downtime, or finality issues.
- **Regulatory risk:** product availability, trading features, yield access, card access, and supported assets may vary by jurisdiction and may change over time.
- **Counterparty and provider risk:** trading venues, liquidity providers, card programs, stablecoin issuers, custodial partners, and other service providers can introduce operational and financial risk.

Tetra should make these risks visible in product, documentation, and onboarding. The product should be simple to use, but not simplistic about risk.
