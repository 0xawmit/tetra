# Tetra Competitive Landing Page Audit

## Decision

Tetra should not copy the usual crypto-card landing page: big APY, cashback, metal card, and a long pile of perks. That category is crowded.

The page should sell a **crypto neobank**: a self-custodial home for a user's digital dollars.

> **Your crypto neobank.**

Trade, Earn, and Spend are the connected jobs of that account. Trade is still a major product reason to care, but it should not make the page feel like a trading terminal.

## The Elements Tetra Should Integrate

### 1. One clear category sentence above the fold

Visitors must understand the product before they understand the features.

**Use:**

> **Your crypto neobank.**

**Supporting line:**

> One self-custodial account to hold digital dollars, trade global markets, earn on eligible idle balances, and spend when you need to.

This makes the category feel immediate and familiar. The supporting line defines what a crypto neobank actually does, without incorrectly claiming that Tetra is a bank.

### 2. A real account visual, not a feature collage

The hero visual should show one balance and three obvious actions:

- **Trade** — supported markets
- **Earn** — eligible idle balance
- **Spend** — card / real-world utility

It should look like a financial home screen, not a trading terminal or a card advertisement. Mine and SoliaPay both make the promise feel more credible by showing the account experience immediately.

### 3. A five-step money flow

The visitor should be able to repeat the product back after one scroll:

> Fund with stablecoins → Trade when opportunity appears → Earn while capital waits → Spend when needed → Return to your account

This is Tetra's differentiating mechanism. Make it a dedicated visual section, not a sentence buried in a feature card.

### 4. A Trade-first product rail

Use three modules in this order: Trade, Earn, Spend. Each module gets one job, one short paragraph, and one product visual or interaction.

Do not introduce transfer, card rewards, referrals, chains, points, protocols, or memberships on the main landing page. Those can live on product detail pages after beta.

### 5. Trust answers before the FAQ

Crypto visitors ask, consciously or not: "Who controls the assets? What actually happens when I use this? Is it available to me?"

Add a short trust band before the waitlist close:

- **Self-custodial by design** — explain only the confirmed custody model.
- **Clear before you act** — show fees, availability, product terms, and risk information before a user commits.
- **Built for a global rollout** — state regions only when confirmed; otherwise say access will vary by location and eligibility.

Avoid invented audit claims, partner logos, licenses, user counts, coverage figures, APYs, or security certifications.

### 6. One CTA with an honest beta expectation

Every primary action should be **Join the beta waitlist**. The CTA should lead to a minimal form: email first, with country/region only if the launch team needs it for rollout planning.

Immediately under the form, say:

> Tetra is preparing for beta. Join to receive launch updates and early-access information. Availability will vary by location and eligibility.

The page should never imply that a visitor can trade, earn, or receive a card today.

## Best-Practice Requirements for Tetra

These are landing-page principles for a high-trust, self-custodial crypto product—not generic SaaS conversion rules.

| Principle | Tetra implementation |
| --- | --- |
| **Clarity before cleverness** | Headline names the account outcome; supporting copy names Trade, Earn, and Spend in plain language. No jargon-only hero. |
| **One primary conversion** | The only primary CTA is the waitlist. Litepaper is a secondary text link, not a competing button. |
| **Status is explicit** | Say beta is coming soon in the hero and CTA close. Clearly label future features as "expected," "planned," or "when available." |
| **Progressive disclosure** | First scroll: what Tetra is and why it matters. Middle: how the loop works. Lower page: custody, availability, FAQs, and disclosures. |
| **Trust is specific** | Describe custody, controls, fees, conditions, and provider roles with confirmed facts; no generic "military-grade security" language. |
| **Self-custody is explained, not just claimed** | A self-custodial product must state what control the user has and the responsibilities/risks that remain. |
| **Yield is transparent** | Say "variable yield" and "eligible balances". Link to a full risk page. Never make a promised return the site’s main conversion engine. |
| **Availability is real** | List supported assets, regions, and partners only when confirmed. Otherwise state that access depends on launch configuration and eligibility. |
| **Mobile is the default** | Preserve hierarchy, form completion, and disclosure readability on a phone. Do not make legal/risk links tiny or hidden. |
| **Fast and legible** | Prioritize the hero message, actual product UI, and CTA over autoplay videos, 3D effects, or a long animated card sequence. |

These align with Stripe’s framing that crypto onboarding must balance clarity, flexibility, and trust; Bitcoin Design’s guidance to make self-custody understandable while educating users about risk; and the FCA’s emphasis on clear, prominent risk information. See [Stripe](https://stripe.com/en-ca/resources/more/crypto-onboarding-best-practices), [Bitcoin Design](https://bitcoin.design/guide/getting-started/principles/), and [FCA guidance](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-and-poor-practice/assessing-compliance-back-end-cryptoasset-financial-promotions-rules).

## Recommended Landing Page Architecture

### 1. Navigation

Logo · How it works · Trade · Earn · Spend · FAQ · **Join the beta waitlist**

Keep the navigation light. Do not add a documentation mega-menu at soft launch.

### 2. Hero: the account promise

**Eyebrow:** SELF-CUSTODIAL GLOBAL FINANCE

**Headline:** Put your crypto to work.

**Supporting copy:** Trade supported global markets, earn on eligible idle balances, and spend when you need to—from one self-custodial account.

**CTA:** Join the beta waitlist

**Below CTA:** Beta coming soon. Availability will vary by location and eligibility.

**Visual:** One Tetra account screen with Trade / Earn / Spend states.

### 3. The broken account experience

**Headline:** Your crypto should work like an account.

Explain the old workflow in one short paragraph: exchange for trades, protocol for yield, card/off-ramp for spending. Then show Tetra's connected loop.

### 4. The Tetra capital loop

**Headline:** Everything your crypto needs in one place.

Use the five-step flow. This is the conceptual centre of the site.

### 5. Product pillars

**Trade first.** Each pillar needs one concrete outcome and one modest product claim. Use a distinct visual for each, but preserve the same account balance throughout the section.

### 6. Custody and transparency

**Headline:** Control without the operating manual.

Explain self-custody in ordinary language. Put detailed disclosures and technical information behind links, but answer the custody question on the page.

### 7. Built for / audience

Use three cards: Active traders, stablecoin holders, global users. Describe the job each person hires Tetra to do, not their demographic identity.

### 8. Beta access

Repeat the waitlist form. Explain what joining means and what will be sent next. This removes the ambiguity common in waitlist pages.

### 9. FAQ + disclosure footer

FAQ should answer: what is Tetra, whether it is live, what can be traded, how Earn works, how Spend works, whether it is a bank, and who controls assets. Footer should link to Litepaper, Terms, Privacy, and Risk Disclosures.

## What the Existing Tetra Draft Should Change

The existing `Landing Page Copy.md` has the right Trade-first logic and disclosure posture. Update it as follows:

1. Add a short beta-status line directly under the hero CTA.
2. Make the capital loop a visual, named section—not just a paragraph within the product story.
3. Add the custody/transparency band before the audience section.
4. Show a consistent account visual across Hero, Trade, Earn, and Spend; the same balance should visibly move through the loop.
5. Keep the "spendable yield" line lower on the page. It is a strong supporting proof point, but it should not outrank Trade in the hero.
6. Do not show unconfirmed APY, partner logos, supported assets, regions, merchant counts, card-network claims, waitlist totals, or security certifications.
7. Keep the product scope tight. No token, points, referrals, credit, AI, chain count, or reward tiers in this launch version.

## Launch Readiness Checklist

- [ ] Headline answers what Tetra is and why it matters in under ten seconds.
- [ ] Every feature claim matches a confirmed beta capability or is explicitly described as planned/expected.
- [ ] One primary CTA is used consistently.
- [ ] Waitlist form works on mobile and has a confirmation state.
- [ ] Product visual shows the account loop, not generic crypto imagery.
- [ ] Custody mechanics are accurately described and reviewed by product/legal.
- [ ] Yield, trading, stablecoin, and card risk disclosures are accessible from the page.
- [ ] Regions, assets, fees, limits, providers, and eligibility are either accurate or deliberately omitted.
- [ ] Litepaper, Terms, Privacy, and Risk Disclosures are live before paid acquisition begins.
- [ ] The page is tested at mobile widths, on slow connections, and with keyboard navigation.
