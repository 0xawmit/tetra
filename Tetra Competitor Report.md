# Tetra Competitor Report

**Research date:** July 30, 2026

## Scope

This report compares Tetra with ten consumer products across wallet, card, banking, trading, and yield. Nine fit Tetra's integration-led model: they package external infrastructure into a unified user experience rather than operating the core protocols behind it.

Hotstuff is included as a deliberate exception. Its **Trade, Invest, Bank** proposition closely overlaps Tetra's intended positioning, but Hotstuff operates a purpose-built Layer 1 powered by its proprietary DracoBFT consensus protocol. The comparison otherwise excludes products such as ether.fi Cash, Gnosis Pay, Plasma One, and Tria because they are tied to a proprietary protocol, network, or orchestration layer. KAST, RedotPay, and Brighty are less exact comparisons because they are not primarily self-custodial, but they remain useful benchmarks for packaging and distribution.

Rates, rewards, availability, and fees vary by region and can change quickly. Figures below reflect public information available on the research date.

## What Their GTM Strategies Show

### 1. [Bleap](https://www.bleap.finance/): ownership plus concrete savings

Bleap does not rely on self-custody as an abstract principle. It turns that principle into clear benefits: no bank in the middle, no advertised FX markup, cashback in USDC, and accessible yield. Its strongest acquisition work makes the value measurable before explaining the technology.

The lesson for Tetra is to attach self-custody to an outcome. "You control your account" is stronger when paired with what the account lets the user do without moving money between apps.

### 2. [KAST](https://www.kast.xyz/): distribution before differentiation

KAST acquired users with a simple global-money story and made the card the habit-forming product. Points, referral competitions, localized promotions, premium cards, and community collaborations gave users repeated reasons to deposit and spend.

The lesson is not to copy its reward complexity. It is to copy the distribution discipline: launch with a clear use case, localize campaigns, reward activated users rather than empty sign-ups, and borrow distribution from communities that already hold stablecoins.

### 3. [Avici](https://avici.money/): enter through one immediate job

Avici's broader vision is an internet neobank, but its market entry is much narrower: hold crypto and obtain spending power without handing over custody. The card and virtual account provide immediate utility while future products expand the relationship.

This is a useful launch model for Tetra. The full account can contain three pillars, but public acquisition still needs one sharp reason to join first.

### 4. [RedotPay](https://www.redotpay.com/): solve access, then build a growth machine

RedotPay focuses on users for whom converting crypto into daily purchasing power is a real problem. Its P2P coverage, card access, remittance, affiliates, and multi-level referral economics are designed for markets where local rails and trusted distribution matter more than crypto ideology.

For Tetra, Southeast Asian expansion will require local distribution and funding routes, not only global product language. Country-level creator and community partnerships could matter more than a broad global campaign.

### 5. [Cadmos Pay](https://pay.cadmos.finance/): broad product, weak wedge

Cadmos is the closest expression of Tetra's product architecture: existing market, yield, payment, and tokenization rails presented through one self-custodial app. Its challenge is that "banking, remittance, DeFi, perps, and tokenized markets" creates a wide story without one obvious reason to switch.

This is the clearest warning for Tetra. A complete product architecture does not automatically create a focused launch narrative.

### 6. [Mine](https://mine.financial/): independence made concrete

Mine positions self-custody through familiar financial objects: a named IBAN, a Mastercard, stocks, and a balance that settles into the user's wallet. The Swiss identity and the line "Your IBAN. Your keys." make control feel credible without leading with blockchain terminology.

Its invite-only model creates scarcity while giving each user a small distribution role. Mine also builds separate acquisition paths for freelancers, digital nomads, and crypto beginners instead of presenting the same generic message to everyone. Tetra can learn from this segmentation, although Mine's absolute claims about balances never being frozen should not be copied without legal and technical review.

### 7. [SoliaPay](https://soliapay.com/): familiar banking with a social layer

SoliaPay removes crypto language from the first interaction and frames the product as simple banking that users own. Username payments and a "social app" experience provide a more human entry point than wallets, chains, and protocols.

Its GTM remains an unproven beta waitlist and leans heavily on up to 12% APY. The useful lesson is its simple language and social payment behavior; the risk is allowing a variable yield number to become the entire reason users join.

### 8. [Exodus Pay](https://www.exodus.com/pay): expand from trusted wallet to daily account

Exodus did not enter the category as a new neobank. It used an existing self-custodial wallet brand and reframed it around sending, spending, and rewards. The product launched inside an app users already trusted, reducing both acquisition cost and the education burden.

Tetra does not have that installed base, so it must borrow trust through partners, communities, and verifiable product details. Exodus also shows that rewards can encourage setup and usage without being described as yield.

### 9. [Brighty](https://brighty.app/): own a customer segment, not only a category

Brighty packages crypto and fiat around concrete users: freelancers receiving international payments, creators earning in crypto, and companies that need cards and payouts. Its content consistently starts with those jobs before introducing the product.

Its referral program pays only after real card activity, while affiliates earn from both card volume and vault activity. That aligns distribution economics with retained usage. For Tetra, this is a stronger model than rewarding waitlist emails or KYC completion alone.

### 10. [Hotstuff](https://www.hotstuff.trade/): trading first, banking second

Hotstuff avoids generic stablecoin-neobank language. It presents itself as the home for capital that trades and invests, with 24/7 global markets and one margin account as the acquisition wedge. Earn and planned banking services then extend the user's relationship with that capital.

Its invite-only beta combines Discord access, points, trading competitions, boosted rewards for RWA markets, and frequent asset launches. The lesson for Tetra is to lead with differentiated market access and use Earn and Spend to deepen retention. However, Hotstuff's owned Layer 1, liquidity infrastructure, and protocol make it a positioning benchmark rather than a like-for-like operating model.

## Implications for Tetra

1. **The trading-first neobank position is no longer empty.** Hotstuff already leads with 24/7 multi-asset markets and tokenized investments under a **Trade, Invest, Bank** narrative. Tetra must distinguish through its self-custodial experience, integration breadth, target markets, and simpler access rather than relying on category language alone.

2. **Tetra should launch the account, but market a wedge.** The strongest initial promise is not "we have Trade, Earn, and Spend." It is the specific outcome that makes the connected account matter. Tetra's strongest candidate is: **trade global markets from digital dollars, while unused capital remains useful and accessible for spending.**

3. **Spend should create habit; Trade should create differentiation.** The card gives users a frequent reason to keep money in Tetra. Global market access gives them a reason to choose Tetra over a card-led stablecoin app.

4. **Do not build the launch around the highest APY or cashback.** Those numbers are expensive, change frequently, and train users to compare Tetra as a commodity. A modest, reliable, clearly funded reward is stronger than a large promotional rate with caps, points, or token conditions.

5. **Use referrals only after activation.** Reward the actions that prove product value: verified account, first deposit, first trade, or first card spend. KAST and RedotPay show that referral systems become powerful when connected to actual usage.

6. **Make the capital loop visible in the product and marketing.** Competitors often place Earn funds in a separate vault or require a transfer before spending. Tetra can differentiate by making one balance, its earning state, its trading deployment, and its spend availability understandable from the main account screen.

## Recommended Launch Read

Tetra should not present itself as another crypto card or as a collection of integrations. It should present itself as the self-custodial account built around active global capital.

The market-facing hierarchy should be:

1. **Trade global markets from digital dollars.**
2. **Keep eligible idle capital productive.**
3. **Use the same account in the real world.**

That gives Tetra a narrower acquisition story than a generic stablecoin neobank while preserving the full Trade, Earn, and Spend vision.
